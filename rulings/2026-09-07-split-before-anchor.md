# Ruling — a `split:` is for two states, not for a scorer's uncertainty

**Decided** by KD on 2026-09-07, on a defect raised during the Pi scoring pass. **Interim**: it settles
the immediate question and leaves the fuller revision of R10 open.

**The defect it answers.** `spectrums/01-scorecard.md` R10 says that where two halves of a harness
disagree, score *"the half the profile states most directly."* On Claude Code that worked — one half
was stated verbatim, the other unanswered. **On Pi it fired five times** (axes I, III, V, VII, IX) and
decided none of them, because Pi states **both** halves equally clearly and by design.

The pattern is one distinction repeated: **posture versus mechanism.** Pi's stated posture is
single-operator, no enforcement, no capture; its shipped mechanism is a shareable settings file, a
`--tools` allowlist and a real promotion path. **A harness whose identity is a published refusal list
will always read this way**, so this is a class of case rather than a one-off.

**The ruling.** **R10 stands, but it is being reached too early.** The axis anchors describe observable
states, and in most apparent splits **one anchor already covers both halves** — so re-read the anchor
before recording anything.

**A `split:` is for two states simultaneously true at different anchors. It is not for a scorer's
uncertainty between them.** That distinction is the whole of this ruling: uncertainty is resolved by
reading more carefully, and a `split:` records a property of the harness, not a property of the read.
