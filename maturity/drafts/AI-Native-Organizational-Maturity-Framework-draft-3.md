**Common fails:**   
Moral panic. fear-driven restrictions that prevent any learning.  
Hero Culture. One person is 3x faster, but when they leave, the capability leaves with them. The mindset is personal productivity, not team capability.  
Prompt theater;  people go through the motions of "using AI" but workflows haven't actually changed. AI is added onto processes rather than redesigning them.  
FOMO vs Innovation.  If you’re chasing AI because “everyone is doing it” you’ll never go past stage 4\.   
Bureaucracy without redesign. Governance layers are added but the underlying work structure doesn't change. AI gets slower because of process overhead, not faster.

A few thoughts that didn’t fit:

🙅‍♀️**AI maturity is not the same thing as AI enthusiasm.**

**💀FOMO vs ✨Innovation mindset.**  If you’re chasing AI because “everyone is doing it” you’ll never get past stage 3\.  

**⚔️Hero Culture.** One person is 3x faster, but individual acceleration is not the same as team capability.

**🚦Stage 3 bottlenecks.** Once a team gets good at AI-assisted work, it often starts generating more output than the org can review, approve, or absorb. This leads to friction, pressure, and token disputes that lead to the commitment cliff. 

**🛑 The AI Commitment Cliff. (Stage 5-6)**  
Unlike NN/g’s UX maturity, getting to AI-first or AI-native is a binary decision that you don’t gradually mature into. Leadership has to decide: **"AI-native workflows are how we work. Not optional. Not experimental.  This is the default."** If you don't commit, you shall not pass.  

**The real shift.  HACD companies will win 2026\.**   
It’s undeniable that technology is moving in this direction.  The stages are there to help you decide what kind of company you want to be, but the companies that adopt HACD first will be the ones pulling ahead. 

The later stages aren’t really about prompts or tools.

They’re about org design: roles, workflows, knowledge systems, incentives, and decision rights.
