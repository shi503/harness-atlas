**Context.** The landscape disagrees about stewardship: one peer names its product after the role, one automates it away as a failure mode, and LoomWarp has nobody. Framing it as a person or a bot forces a choice between two bad options.

**Decision.** Declare an editorial tier in frontmatter, and attach a concurrency model to the tier rather than to a directory or a tool. Low-friction capture is **optimistic** — anyone writes, git merges, pull before edit. Change-managed decisions are **pessimistic** — locks plus a conflict audit, owners only. One corpus, two models, selected by what the content is.

**Consequences.** Capture stays cheap where cheapness matters and safe where safety matters. Stewardship becomes a reviewable property rather than a headcount question.

**Failure of the simpler design.** A single concurrency model over one corpus is wrong at one end or the other: locks make capture too expensive to happen, and optimistic merge makes silent overwrites of ratified decisions possible.
