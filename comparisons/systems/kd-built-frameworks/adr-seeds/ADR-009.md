**Context.** A dispatched agent needs to know what to load, what it may change, and what it must not touch. Prose descriptions of scope are not checkable.

**Decision.** Every PRD enumerates three lists: read-only (context, do not modify), write/modify (the scoped change surface), and create (new files). The manifest is simultaneously a context hint, a scope fence, and the input to the reviewer's diff-scope check.

**Consequences.** Scope creep becomes mechanically detectable — the review layer can ask whether the diff stayed inside the declared surface. Pairs with a hook (see ADR-025) to become binding rather than advisory.

**Failure of the simpler design.** Without an enumerated surface, out-of-scope edits are found by a human reading a diff, which is the review step most likely to be skipped under time pressure.
